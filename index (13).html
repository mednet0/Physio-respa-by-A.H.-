1. What is the primary determinant of lung compliance?
  • A. Surface tension of alveolar fluid
  • B. Elastic recoil of lung tissue and chest wall
  • C. Airway resistance
  • D. Intrapleural pressure
Answer: B. Elastic recoil of lung tissue and chest wall
Lung compliance is determined by the elastic properties of the lung tissue and chest wall. Compliance reflects the ease with which lungs expand, inversely related to elastic recoil. Surface tension affects alveolar stability, not compliance directly. (Guyton, Ch. 38)
2. How does pulmonary surfactant primarily reduce surface tension in alveoli?
  • A. By increasing water molecule cohesion
  • B. By forming a monolayer that disrupts water molecule interactions
  • C. By increasing alveolar fluid viscosity
  • D. By dissolving air molecules in alveolar fluid
Answer: B. By forming a monolayer that disrupts water molecule interactions
Surfactant, produced by type II alveolar cells, forms a monolayer at the air-liquid interface, reducing surface tension by interrupting cohesive forces between water molecules, preventing alveolar collapse. (Guyton, Ch. 38)
3. What is the intrapleural pressure at functional residual capacity (FRC) in a healthy individual?
  • A. +5 cm H₂O
  • B. 0 cm H₂O
  • C. -5 cm H₂O
  • D. -10 cm H₂O
Answer: C. -5 cm H₂O
At FRC, intrapleural pressure is approximately -5 cm H₂O due to the opposing elastic recoil of the lungs and chest wall, maintaining lung expansion. This negative pressure prevents lung collapse. (Guyton, Ch. 37)
4. Which factor most significantly shifts the oxygen-hemoglobin dissociation curve to the right?
  • A. Decreased PCO₂
  • B. Increased pH
  • C. Increased 2,3-DPG
  • D. Decreased temperature
Answer: C. Increased 2,3-DPG
Increased 2,3-DPG (e.g., in hypoxia) reduces hemoglobin’s affinity for oxygen, shifting the curve to the right, facilitating oxygen unloading in tissues. Decreased PCO₂, increased pH, or decreased temperature shift it left. (Guyton, Ch. 39)
5. What is the primary stimulus for the central chemoreceptors in the regulation of respiration?
  • A. Decreased arterial PO₂
  • B. Increased arterial PCO₂
  • C. Decreased arterial pH
  • D. Increased oxygen saturation
Answer: B. Increased arterial PCO₂
Central chemoreceptors in the medulla respond primarily to increased PCO₂, which diffuses into cerebrospinal fluid, forming carbonic acid and lowering pH, stimulating ventilation. (Guyton, Ch. 41)
6. In the absence of surfactant, what would happen to alveolar stability?
  • A. Alveoli would expand uniformly
  • B. Smaller alveoli would collapse into larger ones
  • C. Alveoli would become more compliant
  • D. Alveolar pressure would decrease
Answer: B. Smaller alveoli would collapse into larger ones
Without surfactant, high surface tension causes smaller alveoli to collapse into larger ones due to Laplace’s law (P = 2T/r). Surfactant equalizes surface tension, stabilizing alveoli. (Guyton, Ch. 38)
7. What percentage of CO₂ is transported in the blood as bicarbonate ions?
  • A. 10%
  • B. 30%
  • C. 70%
  • D. 90%
Answer: C. 70%
Approximately 70% of CO₂ is transported as bicarbonate ions (HCO₃⁻) after reacting with water to form carbonic acid, catalyzed by carbonic anhydrase in red blood cells. (Guyton, Ch. 39)
8. What is the primary role of elastic recoil in the lungs during expiration?
  • A. To increase intrapleural pressure
  • B. To drive passive expiration
  • C. To increase alveolar surface tension
  • D. To reduce airway resistance
Answer: B. To drive passive expiration
Elastic recoil of lung tissue and chest wall provides the force for passive expiration by returning the lungs to their resting volume, expelling air without muscle effort. (Guyton, Ch. 37)
9. What happens to the oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve in chronic anemia?
  • A. Shifts to the left due to increased pH
  • B. Shifts to the right due to increased 2,3-DPG
  • C. Remains unchanged
  • D. Shifts to the left due to decreased PCO₂
Answer: B. Shifts to the right due to increased 2,3-DPG
In chronic anemia, increased 2,3-DPG production in red blood cells shifts the oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve to the right, enhancing oxygen delivery to tissues to compensate for reduced oxygen-carrying capacity. (Guyton, Ch. 39)
10. What is the effect of increased airway resistance on lung compliance?

  • B. Decreases compliance
  • C. No effect on compliance
  • D. Causes surfactant deficiency
Answer: C. No effect on compliance
Airway resistance affects airflow but does not directly alter lung compliance, which is determined by elastic properties of the lung and chest wall. (Guyton, Ch. 38)
11. What is the primary source of elastic recoil in the lungs?
  • A. Smooth muscle in the bronchioles
  • B. Collagen and elastin fibers in lung parenchyma
  • C. Surfactant in the alveoli
  • D. Cartilage in the trachea
Answer: B. Collagen and elastin fibers in lung parenchyma
Elastic recoil of the lungs is primarily due to collagen and elastin fibers in the lung parenchyma, which resist stretching and return the lungs to their resting state during expiration. (Guyton, Ch. 38)

  • D. Regulates CO2 levels in the blood
Answer: B. Inhibits inspiration when lungs are overstretched
The Hering-Breuer reflex, mediated by stretch receptors in the lungs, inhibits inspiration when the lungs are overstretched, preventing overinflation. (Guyton, Ch. 41)

Carbonic anhydrase in red blood cells catalyzes the reaction of CO2 with water to form carbonic acid, which dissociates into bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻), the primary form of CO2 transport. (Guyton, Ch. 39)
